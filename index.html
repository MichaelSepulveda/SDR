Hospitality business Strategy & Operations Consultant, alongside a part-time Assistant Manager role Barcelona, 2026 to present

Revenue was falling and nobody could say why.

I joined as part-time Assistant Manager, and the analysis grew into an advisory engagement. Combining point-of-sale, reservation and Google Business Profile data, I found the leak: 20% fewer tables, while spend per ticket had actually risen 7%. The problem was volume, not spend. Guest counts had silently stopped being recorded after a process change. I mapped the acquisition funnel from Google search to reservation, built a business case with opportunity sizing by scenario, and redesigned the metrics and reporting system around tables.

Found the real leak: fewer tables, not lower spend
Papernest Growth Strategy Analyst, internship Barcelona, 2025. French scaleup for home utility and services contracts

Cross-sell wasn't converting, and the easy explanation was that customers didn't want more.

I tested a different hypothesis: we were offering the wrong things to the wrong people. I analysed 30M+ CRM records, built a customer segmentation model and used margin analysis (CM1/CM2) to decide which offers to scale. Then I aligned Sales, Finance and Partnerships across 4 teams and 3 countries around one targeting logic and shared KPIs.

+5% conversion, model adopted by Sales

Questions people ask

The short versions of what usually comes up in a first conversation.

What kind of work are you looking for?

Work where the case and the fix sit close together: a product team deciding what to build, or a traditional business deciding how to modernise. I'm most useful between the analysis and the people who have to act on it, close to the decision rather than downstream from it. Years running restaurant shifts taught me who sits at the end of most decisions: the operator who has to make a new process, tool or target work on a busy night. That's the first test I apply to any idea, including my own.

How do you use AI in your work?

For the repetitive parts, never for the decision. In Tarragona, an n8n and Claude workflow cut a five-day process to under two hours, and it went live only after one pilot proved it. In my restaurant case, AI ran the searches, drafted the structure and played critical reviewers, while I chose which figures to trust and which objections held. What it hasn't sped up is checking its work, so I build that time into every plan.

Why so many sectors, and so many moves?

Most of my roles were built to end: a fixed-term contract, an internship during my master's, a consulting project with a clear scope. The others ended when I chose the next step, like coming to Spain to study. The problem was always the same, though: something quietly holding a business back that nobody had time to look at. Moving taught me to get up to speed fast. What I want now is to stay long enough to see the results compound.

What's something that didn't work?

My first proposal to a restaurant owner was a clear no. The analysis was right, but the fix depended on what he protected most: his team's time. Instead of pushing, I parked it and looked for what we could move without it, starting with the data we already had. It's how I've worked since: when something is stuck, it goes to the backlog, and we iterate on what we can unblock.
